
Hybrid Helium-Electromagnetic Launcher (HHEL)

An open-source, low-cost, human-rated bridge to space

Overview

The Hybrid Helium-Electromagnetic Launcher (HHEL) is a proposed space launch architecture designed to reduce orbital launch costs to €20–€50 ($22–$55) per kilogram while remaining safe and comfortable for human passengers.
By decoupling ground-based electromagnetic acceleration from stratospheric altitude, the system avoids the extreme costs and risks of magnetically levitated launch tubes (StarTram) and eliminates the propellant penalties of traditional chemical rockets.

System Architecture

1. Long-Track Equatorial Ground Acceleration (Human-Rated):
	• Located near the equator (e.g., approaching Mount Kenya or the Ecuadorian Andes) to gain ~465 m/s (1,670 km/h) from Earth's rotation and avoid severe jet streams.
	• A 110 to 160 km ground track runs across flat terrain and curves gently up a 5–6 km mountain slope.
	• Extending the track length keeps acceleration at a gentle 2G to 3G over 4.5 to 6.5 minutes, making it suitable for untrained human passengers as well as cargo.
	• Powered by slow-charging, fast-discharging energy storage (flywheels or supercapacitors) fed by local renewable energy.
2. Balloon-Suspended Stratospheric Glide Tube:
	• At the mountain summit (~6 km altitude), the capsule enters a lightweight, passive glide tube extending to 20–25 km altitude (above 95%+ of Earth's atmosphere).
	• Because acceleration is already complete, this elevated section requires no heavy magnets or power cables. It is suspended by stratospheric helium balloons and anchored with high-tensile guy-wires.
3. Low-Pressure Helium Atmosphere (Zero-Vacuum Design):
	• Instead of maintaining a hard vacuum—which risks structural implosion—the entire tube is filled with low-pressure helium (matching ambient pressure at 20 km).
	• Structural Integrity: Internal gas pressure counteracts external atmospheric pressure, allowing an ultra-lightweight carbon-composite and thin aluminum liner construction.
	• Buoyancy: The helium provides internal lift, reducing the load on the suspension balloons.
	• Low Drag & Heating: Helium's low density and high speed of sound minimize shockwaves and aerodynamic heating at ~7.8 km/s. A frangible foil membrane seals the upper exit.
4. Orbital Insertion:
	• Exiting at ~25 km altitude at near-orbital velocity, the capsule uses a compact kick-stage rocket (10%–15% of total mass) to circularize its orbit, enabling up to 80% payload mass fraction.
